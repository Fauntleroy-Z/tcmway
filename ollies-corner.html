One Pendant.
A Thousand Journeys.

The same yin-yang pendant. The same owl. Reimagined through the brushstrokes of Kyoto, the stained glass of Notre-Dame, the geometry of Marrakech — and other worlds yet to come.

I've worn this pendant through ink and stone, across silk roads and stained-glass light. Let me show you what I saw.

— Ollie
A temple in Kyoto
Sumi-e ink wash · Edo period · 1688
The brush paused. I held my breath. So did the mountain.
Notre-Dame de Paris
Gothic stained glass · Before the fire
Light doesn't just pass through stained glass. It confesses. Every color told me a different secret.
The Silk Road
Persian miniature · Somewhere between Dunhuang and Samarkand
A merchant offered me tea. A monk offered me silence. I accepted both.
The cave of Lascaux
Charcoal on limestone · 17,000 years ago
Before words. Before temples. Before medicine. Someone still drew a circle and knew it meant wholeness.
A study in Bloomsbury
Literary illustration · London, the winter Woolf wrote Orlando
She looked up from her manuscript and said, "Stay a while." So I perched. For a hundred pages.
The souk of Marrakech
Islamic geometric art · An hour before dawn
Geometry that makes your eyes dance. I got dizzy trying to count the stars in this tilework. (There are 147. I checked.)

The pendant has more journeys ahead. I haven't been to Dunhuang yet. Or Udaipur. Or anywhere with a good cup of tea and a blank wall.

— Ollie, signing off

The pendant returns home.
