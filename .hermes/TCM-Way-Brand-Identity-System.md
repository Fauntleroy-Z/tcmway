## 1. Brand Philosophy & Positioning

### Who We Are

TCM Way is not a wellness brand. It is not a clinic. It is not selling herbs, treatments, or cures.

It is a **translation project**: taking 2,000 years of Chinese medical and philosophical wisdom and rendering it in plain, warm, contemporary English — like a friend explaining something over tea.

### Brand Voice

- **Warm, not mystical** — no incense, no gongs, no "ancient secrets"
- **Human, not clinical** — like Calvin & Hobbes on a good day, not a medical textbook
- **Curious, not preachy** — "here's something interesting" not "here's what you should believe"
- **Grounded, not esoteric** — every idea connects back to something a reader can feel in their own body

### Brand Architecture

```
        ┌──────────────────────────┐
        │   YIN-YANG PENDANT LOGO  │  ← Super-symbol. Appears everywhere.
        │   (The Brand Anchor)     │
        └────────────┬─────────────┘
                     │
      ┌──────────────┼──────────────┐
      ▼              ▼              ▼
 ┌─────────┐  ┌────────────┐  ┌─────────┐
 │ TCM WAY │  │EAST & INNER│  │  OLLIE  │
 │  Blog   │  │  Podcast   │  │ Social  │
 │         │  │            │  │   IP    │
 └─────────┘  └────────────┘  └─────────┘
```

**The pendant is the anchor.** Ollie the owl is a content character — he lives in comics, speaks in articles, and appears on social cards. But the **brand mark is the pendant**, not the owl. The owl is the voice; the pendant is the identity.

### Taglines

| Channel | Tagline | Purpose |
|---------|---------|---------|
| Blog | *"Understand your body in a language 2,000 years old."* | Bridges ancient knowledge with modern comprehension |
| Podcast | *"From the East. For your Inner."* | Play on "East & Inner" — geography + introspection |

## 2. Logo Concept: The Yin-Yang Pendant

### The Core Idea

Most yin-yang symbols are **abstract philosophy delivered in vector perfection** — cold, geometric, distant. They say: *"This is Eastern wisdom. You may admire it from afar."*

The TCM Way pendant is different. It is a **physical object that has been lived with** — worn against skin, handled by fingers, passed through generations. It says: *"This belongs to someone. It has been used. It can be yours too."*

### Design Specifications

#### Shape & Proportions

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Outer circle | **Hand-drawn, slightly asymmetrical.** Diameter varies by 2-3% around the circumference. Not a perfect circle — this is the most important design decision |
| S-curve | A flowing S-line dividing the circle into two interlocking halves. **Visible brushstroke wobble** — the line trembles slightly, like a calligrapher's hand, not a compass |
| Dots | Two small dots, one in each half. Placed where the eye naturally rests — not mechanically centered. Slight ink pooling at the edges |
| Ring & Cord | A **thin string/cord passes through a small ring** at the top of the pendant. The cord hangs naturally, with a subtle curve. It is a **real object**, not an abstraction |
| Proportions | The pendant fills ~70-80% of the composition. Generous negative space around it. The cord extends above, suggesting it's being held or worn |

#### Stroke Style

- **Ink-brush quality**: Strokes have variable width — thicker where the brush presses, thinner where it lifts
- **Slight ink bleed**: Edges are not razor-sharp. There's a micro-texture of ink bleeding into paper fibers
- **Warm brown ink** (#3B2A1A), not pure black. Black feels like a philosophy textbook. Brown feels like a letter from a friend
- **No outlines**: The form emerges from filled brushstrokes, not hollow contour lines

#### How Imperfection Is Achieved Intentionally

| Technique | Visual Effect |
|-----------|---------------|
| Slightly oval circle | Feels drawn by hand, not generated by software |
| Trembling S-curve | Evokes brush calligraphy — "the beauty of the imperfect line" (侘寂) |
| Variable ink density | Some areas are darker, some lighter — like a real brush loaded with ink |
| Asymmetric dot placement | Feels organic, not machine-aligned |
| Natural cord drape | Creates a sense of weight and gravity — this is a *thing*, not a *diagram* |

#### The "Worn Object" Quality

The pendant should look like it has **a history**. Not distressed or damaged — but **lived-in**. Like a family heirloom that has been handled with care for decades:

- Very subtle warm patina around the edges
- Slight color variation across the ink (not dead-flat fill)
- The paper/background has the warmth of aged cream, not sterile white

The color system is built around **warmth and groundedness**. No bright pharmaceutical blues. No mystical purples. No cold clinical whites. The palette evokes: aged paper, warm ink, autumn leaves, candlelight, earth after rain.

| ██ | `#FDF6EE` | `--paper` | Warm Cream | **Background color.** The paper beneath the pendant. Site-wide background. |
| ██ | `#B83A2A` | `--accent` | Cinnabar Red | **Brand accent.** Ollie's ear tufts, links, emphasis. Used sparingly for impact. |

| ██ | `#1A1410` | `--deepest` | Deepest Brown | Pupils, extreme emphasis. Used very sparingly. |

### Neutral Palette (for content)

| Swatch | Hex Code | Role |
|--------|----------|------|
| ██ | `#FDF6EE` | Page background (`--paper`) |
| ██ | `#FAF3E8` | Card backgrounds, subtle elevation (`--light`) |

| ██ | `#8A7E6E` | Subtle text, captions (`--subtle`) |
| ██ | `#3B2A1A` | Body text, headings (`--ink`) |

### Color Proportions (60-30-10 Rule)

```
60%  Warm Cream (#FDF6EE) — background, breathing room
30%  Deep Brown (#3B2A1A) — text, lines, structure
10%  Cinnabar Red (#B83A2A) — accent, emphasis, energy
```

### What We Avoid

| Color | Why We Don't Use It |
|-------|---------------------|
| Pure white `#FFFFFF` | Clinical, sterile. Feels like a hospital |
| Pure black `#000000` | Harsh, philosophical. Feels like a textbook |
| Bright greens | "Wellness influencer" aesthetic |
| Purples / magentas | Mystical / "spiritual guru" look |
| Electric blues | Pharmaceutical / corporate |
| Neons | Wrong century |

### Dark Mode Considerations

For dark mode readers: the cream background inverts to a warm dark brown (`#1A1410` or similar), text inverts to cream. The pendant logo should be provided in a **light-on-dark variant** (cream ink on dark background) for dark mode headers.

## 5. Typography

### Design Philosophy

The pendant is hand-drawn. The typography must honor that. A computer-perfect geometric sans-serif next to a trembling brushstroke would feel like a betrayal.

**Rule**: Every letterform in the brand system should carry evidence of human hands.

### Primary Wordmark: Brush Calligraphy ("TCM WAY")

| Property | Specification |
|----------|---------------|
| Style | Hand-brushed, flowing ink strokes |
| Character | Variable thickness — thick where the brush presses, thin where it lifts |
| Texture | Slight ink bleed at edges, organic pooling at stroke endpoints |
| Spacing | Generous — letters breathe independently |
| Weight | Medium-bold, with natural stroke variation |
| Language | Latin alphabet rendered with Chinese calligraphy energy |

This is the **custom wordmark**, not a font. It is hand-rendered for the brand mark. It cannot be typed — it is drawn each time, ideally from the same master.

**Font recommendations that approximate the feel** (for secondary text when the custom wordmark can't be used):

| Font | Character | Where to Get |
|------|-----------|-------------|
| **Mrs Eaves** | Warm, humanist serif. Slightly imperfect letterforms. | Commercial (Emigre) |
| **Baskerville** | Classic, warm serif. Readable, timeless, not cold. | System font |
| **Calluna** | Serif with visible stroke variation. Friendly. | Commercial (exljbris) |
| **Brioso Pro** | Calligraphic serif with brush-like energy. | Adobe Fonts |
| **Chaparral Pro** | Hybrid slab-serif. Warm, approachable, excellent readability. | Adobe Fonts |

### Podcast Wordmark: Sign Painter ("EAST & INNER")

| Property | Specification |
|----------|---------------|
| Style | Hand-painted casual lettering |
| Character | Slightly uneven baseline, varying letter widths |
| Mood | Friendly, approachable, "come in, we're open" |
| Spacing | Tighter than blog — letters feel like they belong together |

**Font recommendations for secondary podcast text**:

| Font | Character |
|------|-----------|
| **Brioso Pro** | Calligraphic, hand-drawn energy |
| **Satisfy** | Casual script, free on Google Fonts |
| **Homemade Apple** | Warm hand-lettered feel |
| **Caveat** | Natural handwriting, free on Google Fonts |

### Body Text: Reading Font

For long-form articles, the body text font must prioritize **readability** without sacrificing warmth:

| Property | Specification |
|----------|---------------|
| Primary | **Georgia** — warm serif, system font, excellent screen readability |
| Fallback | `'Times New Roman', serif` |
| Size | 18-20px for article body |
| Line height | 1.7-1.8 for comfortable reading |
| Color | `#3B2A1A` (warm brown, not black) |

**Why Georgia?** It's a system font — no loading, no FOUT. It was designed for screen reading. It has warmth: the serifs are friendly, not severe. It pairs naturally with hand-drawn elements because it doesn't pretend to be perfect.

### Chinese Text

For Chinese language content, characters embedded in designs, or bilingual layouts:

| Font | Use Case |
|------|----------|
| **霞鹜文楷 (LXGW WenKai)** | Primary Chinese display. Open-source KaiTi style — brush energy, warm, legible |
| **Noto Serif CJK** | Fallback. Reliable, comprehensive |

### Typography Hierarchy

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  CUSTOM WORDMARK (hand-drawn, unique)       │  ← Brand name, podcast name
│  Georgia Bold (serif, system font)          │  ← Article titles, section heads
│  Georgia Regular (serif, system font)       │  ← Body text, paragraphs
│  Georgia Italic (serif, system font)        │  ← Quotes, emphasis, Ollie speaks
│  Georgia / mono (small, subtle)             │  ← Captions, meta, dates
└─────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Why Not Sans-Serif?

| Serif (Georgia) | Sans-Serif (Helvetica, Inter, etc.) |
|-----------------|-------------------------------------|
| Feels like a book — warm, human | Feels like a startup website — clean, cold |
| Matches hand-drawn pendant (variation) | Clashes with hand-drawn pendant (perfection) |
| 2,000-year tradition of movable type | 20th-century industrial aesthetic |
| Western readers associate serif with *editorial trust* | Western readers associate sans-serif with *corporate efficiency* |

For TCM Way, trust > efficiency.

## 6. Usage Guidelines

### Minimum Sizes

| Variant | Minimum Width | Notes |
|---------|:------------:|-------|
| Pure Pendant (favicon) | 16px | At 16px the S-curve must still be visible |
| Pure Pendant (social anchor) | 48px | Cord and ring discernible |
| Blog Lockup (pendant + text) | 120px | "TCM WAY" letters legible |
| Podcast Lockup (pendant + text) | 180px | "EAST & INNER" letters legible |

### Clear Space

The pendant requires breathing room. The minimum clear space around any logo variant is **equal to the height of the pendant** (or 50% of the total lockup height).

```
┌────────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                        │
│           ← CLEAR SPACE →              │
│                                        │
│         ┌──────────────┐              │
│         │              │              │
│         │   PENDANT    │              │  ← Clear space = pendant height
│         │              │              │
│         └──────────────┘              │
│                                        │
│           ← CLEAR SPACE →              │
│                                        │
└────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Background Rules

| Background | Variant to Use | Notes |
|------------|---------------|-------|
| Warm Cream `#FDF6EE` | ✅ All variants | Native. Best. |
| Light colors (pastels) | ✅ All variants | Pendant reads clearly |
| White or near-white | ⚠️ All variants | Acceptable, but prefer cream |
| Dark backgrounds | ⚠️ Inverted variant | Use cream-ink-on-dark version |
| Photographs | ✅ Pure pendant (C) | Place in a calm area of the image |
| Busy patterns | ❌ Do not use | Pendant needs clean background |
| Gradient backgrounds | ⚠️ Caution | Only if gradient is subtle and warm |

### What NOT to Do

| ❌ Don't | ✅ Do |
|----------|------|
| Stretch or compress the pendant | Scale proportionally, maintaining aspect ratio |
| Change the pendant color to anything but `#3B2A1A` | Use only the approved ink color |
| Add drop shadows, glows, or effects | Keep the pendant flat and ink-like |
| Place the pendant inside a shape (circle, badge) | Let it float in its own space |
| Use the owl as the brand logo | Owl = content character, pendant = brand mark |
| Combine the pendant with other symbols | The pendant stands alone |
| Use vector-perfect replacement | Imperfection is the brand's visual signature |
| Rotate the pendant | Pendant is always upright (ring at top, cord hanging) |
| Add text inside the pendant circle | Text belongs outside the pendant |
| Put the pendant on a photo without clear space | Always maintain breathing room |

### File Format Guidelines

| Use Case | Format | Notes |
|----------|--------|-------|
| Web (photos, articles) | PNG, RGB | 72dpi, optimized |
| Print (merch, cards) | PNG/PDF, CMYK | 300dpi, from full-size master |
| Favicon | PNG or ICO | 32×32px, use `favicon.png` |
| Social media | PNG | 80×80px anchor, use `pendant-social-anchor.png` |
| SVG | ❌ **Do not use** | SVG loses hand-drawn texture and ink bleed. PNG preserves warmth |

### Social Media Application

On social cards (Twitter/X, Facebook, LinkedIn, Pinterest), the pure pendant (Variant C) appears in the **bottom-right corner** at 60-80px:

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────┐
│                                     │
│    Social card image content        │
│    (Ollie comic, article graphic,   │
│     or cultural illustration)       │
│                                     │
│                          ┌────┐     │
│                          │ 吊 │     │  ← Pendant anchor, 80px
│                          └────┘     │
└─────────────────────────────────────┘
```

This creates a consistent **brand watermark** across all platforms without competing with the card's content.

## 7. Ollie Integration: Owl + Pendant Coexistence

### The Relationship

Ollie the owl and the yin-yang pendant are **separate but connected**:

| | Ollie (Content Character) | Pendant (Brand Mark) |
|---|---|---|
| **Role** | Storyteller, guide, voice | Identity, recognition, trust |
| **Where it appears** | Article comics, Ollie Speaks boxes, about page, social cards | Header, footer, favicon, watermark |
| **Visual style** | Warm hand-drawn vector/PNG, full color | Monochrome ink, warm brown |
| **Relationship to reader** | "Hi, I'm Ollie. Let me explain." | "You're in the right place." |
| **Can it change?** | Yes — different art styles, expressions, poses | No — pendant is invariant |

### The Design Logic

**Ollie wears the pendant.** In his character design, Ollie has a gold yin-yang pendant around his neck. This creates a visual bridge:

```
Reader sees pendant on the site →  "This is TCM Way"
Reader sees pendant on Ollie   →  "Ollie is part of TCM Way"
Reader sees pendant on social  →  "This came from TCM Way"
```

The pendant is the **thread that connects everything**. Ollie is the person wearing it.

### Where They Appear Together

| Location | Ollie | Pendant | Notes |
|----------|:-----:|:-------:|-------|
| Website header | ❌ | ✅ | Pure pendant + "TCM WAY" |
| Article comics | ✅ | ✅ | Ollie wears the pendant as part of his design |
| Ollie Speaks box | ✅ (small) | ❌ | Ollie mini avatar only; pendant is in the header |
| Podcast cover | ✅ (color) | ✅ | Ollie as hero image; pendant + "EAST & INNER" as lockup |
| Social cards | ✅ (various art) | ✅ (corner) | Ollie is the content; pendant is the watermark |
| Favicon | ❌ | ✅ | Pure pendant — too small for owl |
| About page | ✅ (hero) | ✅ (header) | Ollie portrait; pendant in header navigation |
| Merch | ❌ or ✅ | ✅ | Pendant on its own or Ollie wearing pendant |

### Separation Rules

1. **The pendant never appears inside Ollie's body** (it's not a chest tattoo — it's jewelry around his neck)
2. **Ollie's pendant is gold** (`#C9A84C`) — distinct from the brand pendant's brown ink (`#3B2A1A`)
3. **Ollie never replaces the brand pendant** in header/favicon/social anchor positions
4. **The brand pendant is never anthropomorphized** — no eyes, no face, no character

### Art Style Consistency for Ollie

Ollie's species and signature details are fixed:

| Element | Specification |
|---------|---------------|
| Species | **Screech Owl** (*Megascops asio*) — prominent red ear tufts |
| Ear tufts | `#B83A2A` (Cinnabar Red) — must be visible in every depiction |
| Body | Warm apricot gradient (`#F5D0A8` → `#E8A87C` → `#C07850`) |
| Eyes | Large yellow/orange with radial gradient, eyelid creases |
| Beak | Short curved, `#B83A2A` with gold accent line |
| Pendant | Gold yin-yang (`#C9A84C`) around neck |
| Blush | Warm apricot (`#E8A87C`, opacity 0.35) on cheeks |

**Social card strategy**: Ollie can appear in **different art styles** (watercolor, woodcut, ink wash, pencil sketch) on social media cards — but the **corner pendant watermark** ties everything back to the brand. Ollie is the canvas; the pendant is the signature.

- Recognizable to anyone with passing familiarity with Chinese culture (East or West)

- Warm and approachable when rendered as a worn object (not a sterile diagram)
- Simple enough to work at favicon size

## 9. The Design Logic: Why This Pendant Is Different

When asked "why did you design it this way?" — here is the answer.

### Comparison: TCM Way Pendant vs. Standard Yin-Yang Symbol

| Dimension | Standard Yin-Yang | TCM Way Pendant |
|-----------|-------------------|-----------------|
| **Geometry** | Vector-perfect circle, mathematically symmetric | Hand-drawn, slightly asymmetrical |
| **Lines** | Clean, mechanical S-curve | Brushstroke with visible tremble |
| **Material** | Abstract symbol on a flat surface | A physical object: ink on paper |
| **Context** | Floating in philosophical space | Hanging from a cord — being worn |
| **Temperature** | Cold (mathematical, distant) | Warm (human, touched, handled) |
| **Relationship** | "This is a concept you should understand" | "This is something someone loves" |
| **Cultural signal** | "Eastern philosophy — admire from distance" | "A friend's necklace — come closer" |

### The Three Design Decisions That Define It

#### Decision 1: Imperfection Over Precision

Western audiences are drowning in vector-perfect "wellness" logos — mandalas, lotus flowers, geometric chakras. All machine-made, all interchangeable.

A hand-drawn line with a slight tremble is instantly recognizable as **human**. It cuts through the noise because it doesn't look like everything else.

#### Decision 2: Object Over Symbol

A yin-yang diagram says "philosophy class." A yin-yang pendant on a cord says "someone wears this every day." The cord is the design element that transforms it from abstract to personal.

The psychological effect: seeing a worn object triggers a different response than seeing a symbol. An object invites curiosity ("what is that?"). A symbol invites categorization ("oh, that's yin-yang").

#### Decision 3: Brown Over Black

Black ink on white paper = textbook. Brown ink on cream paper = letter.

The color decision (`#3B2A1A` on `#FDF6EE`) deliberately evokes:
- Aged manuscript paper
- Tea-stained pages
- Handwritten correspondence
- Warmth, not authority

These three decisions together create a brand mark that says: **"This knowledge is old, but it's alive, and it belongs to people — not to institutions."**

### What It Communicates to a Western Reader

| What they might think | What we want them to feel |
|-----------------------|--------------------------|
| "This looks ancient" | → "This looks *trustworthy*" |
| "This looks hand-drawn" | → "This looks *personal*" |
| "This looks worn" | → "This looks *tested by time*" |
| "What is that symbol?" | → "I want to learn more" |

### Historical Reference: Calvin & Hobbes Ink-Wash Aesthetic

The founder has cited Calvin & Hobbes' warm ink-wash newspaper comic sensibility as a reference. This is instructive:

- Bill Watterson's Sunday strips used watercolor washes, not digital flat color
- The line art has visible brush variation — thick-to-thin strokes
- The overall feel is **warm, handmade, intimate** — a stark contrast to the vector-flat aesthetic of modern web design

The pendant shares this DNA: it looks like it was made by a person using a brush, not a machine using Bezier curves.

### Design Principles (Living Document)

1. **The pendant is sacred.** It never changes. It is the single source of brand truth.
2. **Imperfection is intentional.** If it looks like a computer made it, it's wrong.
3. **Warmth over authority.** Brown over black. Cream over white. Hand over machine.
4. **Ollie wears the pendant, not the other way around.** The owl serves the symbol, not the reverse.
5. **Text can change; the mark cannot.** Blog, podcast, future ventures — same pendant, different words around it.
6. **Less is more.** The pendant doesn't need decoration, effects, or company. It stands alone.

## Appendix C: Design Brief — For Future Designers

If you're a designer hired to create assets for TCM Way, here's what you need to know:

### The Non-Negotiables

1. **The pendant circle is NOT a perfect circle.** If you draw it with the Circle tool, you've already failed.
2. **The S-curve has a visible hand tremble.** Not sloppy — intentional. Like a calligrapher's controlled imperfection.
3. **The ink color is #3B2A1A, not #000000.** Brown, not black. Always.
4. **The background is #FDF6EE, not #FFFFFF.** Cream, not white. Always.
5. **The cord is real.** It has weight. It curves naturally. It's not a decorative flourish — it's the detail that makes the pendant an object.

### What You Can Play With

- Text treatments around the pendant (for new channels/products)
- Ollie's art style in social cards (different artistic movements)
- Complementary colors for specific campaigns (keep the palette as reference)

### Reference Images

- Calvin & Hobbes Sunday strips (ink wash warmth)
- Chinese calligraphy on rice paper (brush texture)
- Worn silver jewelry (object quality, patina)
- Aged manuscripts (cream paper, brown ink)
