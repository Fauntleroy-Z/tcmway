TCM Way publishes educational material about Chinese medicine, culture, and everyday life for an English-speaking audience. This page explains the standards behind that material.

TCM Way is not a medical service. Ollie is not a physician and does not diagnose, prescribe, sell herbs, or provide individual medical care. Nothing on this site is medical advice or a substitute for assessment by a qualified healthcare professional.

Authorship

Ollie is the author's pen name. Behind it is one real person: a Chinese writer and international-trade professional who has studied Chinese medicine independently, with a particular interest in the Shanghan Lun (《伤寒论》) and its six-channel framework. The author has passed China's national qualification examination for TCM pharmacist (执业中药师资格考试). That qualification is not a medical licence, and the author does not present it as one.

TCM Way does not invent clinicians, medical reviewers, patients, spiritual teachers, or coaching credentials. Published bylined work remains the author's responsibility even when AI tools have assisted the production process.

Topic selection

Articles are developed from a maintained topic pool and checked against existing coverage before drafting. The editorial mix keeps Chinese medicine and the body as the main subject while allowing connected material on food, seasons, thought, practice, and ordinary Chinese life.

Topics are not selected from keyword volume alone. We prioritise useful questions, clear gaps in the existing library, corrections to misleading material, and subjects that can support a durable reading path rather than a one-off page.

Sources and evidence

When possible, articles distinguish:

  • what a classical or historical text says;
  • what modern research has examined and what its limits are;
  • what is a cultural interpretation or a writer's observation.

Classical origin is not evidence of clinical effectiveness. A modern study is not automatically evidence that a broad traditional category maps onto a biomedical disease. Readers should treat every source according to the claim it can actually support.

Medical and safety boundaries

We avoid diagnosis, individual prescribing, treatment promises, and statements that imply a traditional pattern can replace emergency care, testing, vaccination, medication, or other clinically indicated treatment. Articles may describe what traditions say people historically noticed or did; that is not an instruction to self-treat.

When a symptom can have urgent or serious causes, the article should say so plainly. Herbs, moxibustion, cupping, and other practices can carry risks. A web article cannot assess an individual's condition.

Use of AI

AI tools may assist with research organisation, drafting, language editing, structural checks, and quality testing. They are not treated as sources of truth and are not presented as human authors, patients, doctors, or reviewers.

Factual claims, source interpretation, medical boundaries, and final editorial responsibility remain with the publication. AI-assisted text is reviewed against the same standards as any other draft.

Editorial review

A draft is checked for structure, factual consistency, source handling, natural English, repeated AI-style phrasing, medical boundaries, and the difference between tradition and evidence. A simulated reader panel may be used to find clarity and momentum problems, but it is never described as real human testimony or independent clinical review.

No article is treated as medically reviewed unless a genuinely qualified reviewer has actually reviewed it and that fact can be documented. TCM Way does not manufacture that badge.

Independence and disclosure

Paid placements, affiliate relationships, sponsorships, or commercial interests that could affect a recommendation should be disclosed clearly. TCM Way does not sell individualised medical advice.

Corrections and updates

Living articles may be updated when a source changes, a factual error is found, a safety boundary needs to be clearer, or the presentation can be improved without changing the intended meaning. Material corrections should be noted in the article or its update history.

To report an error, use the contact page. Please include the article URL, the disputed sentence, and a source when available.

Questions and privacy

Reader questions can help identify useful topics, but receiving a message does not create a clinical relationship. Do not send urgent medical information through the site. Read the Privacy Policy for data-handling details and the Medical Disclaimer for the full use boundary.
