What is inside

A map, not a canon.

Reading order matters. Start with orientation and history. Approach primary texts after you know what kind of book you are holding. Treat translations as interpretations, not interchangeable window glass.

  1. The Web That Has No WeaverTed J. Kaptchuk · An accessible first orientation to Chinese medical reasoning.
  2. Medicine in China: A History of IdeasPaul U. Unschuld · The historical frame that prevents “ancient China” from becoming one undifferentiated idea.
  3. Huang Di Nei Jing Su WenThe Yellow Emperor’s Inner Classic, Basic Questions · Read with a named translation and historical notes.
  4. Huang Di Nei Jing Ling ShuThe Spiritual Pivot · The companion classic, especially useful for acupuncture and channel discussions.
  5. Nan JingThe Classic of Difficult Issues · A compact classical text shaped by commentary and interpretation.
  6. Shang Han LunZhang Zhongjing · Treatise on Cold Damage: one pillar of formula literature and clinical reasoning.
  7. Jin Gui Yao LueZhang Zhongjing · Essential Prescriptions of the Golden Cabinet: a broader clinical companion.
  8. Ben Cao Gang MuLi Shizhen · The Compendium of Materia Medica: world-historical scale, not a modern safety manual.
  9. Wen Bing Tiao BianWu Tang · Systematic Differentiation of Warm Diseases: a later tradition with its own logic and vocabulary.
  10. A Flourishing YinCharlotte Furth · Gender, medicine, and history in China, 960–1665: a vital check on universalizing claims.
