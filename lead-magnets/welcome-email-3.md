Here's how it works: every organ system gets a two-hour window when it's at peak operation. Two hours to process. Two hours to repair. Two hours to handle exactly its job. Then it hands off to the next organ, like a relay race. All day. All night. No weekends.

And here's why this matters for sleep — specifically for you:

That 3 AM wake-up you keep having? It's not random. 1–3 AM is Liver time. This is when your body is supposed to be processing the day's emotional residue and doing its deepest internal cleanup. Wake up at 2:30 AM with your mind spinning and a weird heat in your chest? Your Liver is raising a flag. It's saying: I can't finish my shift. There's still stuff sitting here from yesterday.

The ginger tea I mentioned in the last email? In organ-clock language, it warms the digestive center — Stomach and Spleen channels. When those channels have enough warmth to do their jobs during the day, the Liver doesn't have to moonlight at 2 AM picking up their slack. Suddenly a bedtime tea isn't just a bedtime tea.

That brain fog that hits you every day at 2 PM? Small Intestine time — your body is sorting nutrients from lunch, and your brain is waiting for the shipment. Evening irritability around 7 PM? Stomach time — your digestive fire is supposed to be roaring, and the pilot light is out.
