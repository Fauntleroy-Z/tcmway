This is your herb for the very beginning of a cold. That moment where you're not fully sick yet, but you KNOW something's coming. Chills. A stiff neck. Maybe a slight sweat that doesn't seem to help. Okay, the sun is nice. But the core of the house is still cold. Time for a space heater.

Dried ginger puts a space heater right in the kitchen. It reignites your digestive fire so you can actually process food and generate warmth for the whole house.

Now we're in serious territory. It's the dead of winter. The power is out. The pilot light on the main furnace — in the basement — has gone out. The whole house is frigid. No sweater helps.

Prepared aconite dives into the basement and relights the pilot light.

Cinnamon twig primarily addresses external cold invasion. A chill that just got in from outside. Dried ginger and prepared aconite address internal cold from a deeper, chronic pattern where the body isn't generating enough heat on its own.

They're related — a body with weak internal warmth is more vulnerable to external invasion — but they're not the same thing. Think of it like this.
