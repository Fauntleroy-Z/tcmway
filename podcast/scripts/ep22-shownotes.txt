Ep22: Appetite Is Your Body's Health Barometer

Black coffee at seven can quiet an empty stomach for an hour. By three in the afternoon, your hands are shaking over the keyboard and you are hunting for crackers, chocolate, or anything that will stop the noise. That sudden scramble is not a failure of willpower. It is an overdue bill.

This episode uses a small owl on an oak limb to examine appetite as a signal. In the traditional stomach-and-spleen model, the stomach receives food while the spleen transforms it and carries nourishment onward. The Huangdi Neijing calls them the granary officials. Later physicians connected their digestive warmth with the deeper Mingmen fire, and Li Dongyuan made the middle of the body central to health.

Then the clean map gets messy. We walk through four patterns, not four labels: low appetite with fatigue, low appetite with Dampness, early fullness, and excess hunger. We also separate stomach fire from stomach yin deficiency, look at what cravings can and cannot tell you, and examine why stress makes some people lose their appetite while others overeat.

The episode closes with a seven-day appetite audit, three ordinary practices, and a clear safety boundary. Persistent appetite loss, unexplained weight loss, difficulty swallowing, vomiting, blood, black stools, severe pain, fluid loss, or confusion deserve medical attention. This is a traditional framework for paying attention to a signal. It is not a diagnosis from a single symptom.
