In Chinese, this day is called Li Qiu [lee chyoh]. It means the Beginning of Autumn. The Chinese calendar doesn't wait for leaves to change color. It follows the sun's path. Ancient astronomers divided that path into twenty-four equal parts. Each part is a solar term. When the sun crosses into a new segment, a new phase begins.

August seventh is one of those markers. The weather may still feel like summer. But the energy has already shifted.

Now, the twenty-four solar terms are not superstition. They are based on careful astronomy. The astronomers watched the sun's movement through the year. They divided the full circle into twenty-four segments. Each segment is fifteen degrees. That may sound technical. But the simple part is this. Each small shift in the sun's position changes the amount of daylight. It changes the quality of heat. It changes how plants and animals behave. Farmers used this system for thousands of years. They knew when to plant. They knew when to transplant. They knew when to harvest. They knew when to rest. One term told them to fix the plows. Another said the rice seedlings were ready to move to the paddies. A third marked the day to bring the grain in. The system works so well that it became a UNESCO Intangible Cultural Heritage. That means the whole world has recognized it as something precious. It's not an old folk tale. It's a working calendar.

Li Qiu [lee chyoh] means the peak of yang has passed. Yang is the hot, expanding force. From this day forward, yin begins to rise. Yin is the cooling, contracting, storing force.
