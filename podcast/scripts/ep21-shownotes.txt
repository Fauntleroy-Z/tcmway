Ep21: Cold Feet Aren't Just Bad Circulation

Your sweater feels warm, but your toes feel like two cubes of river ice. Under a blanket, your chest warms while your heels stay cold. That mismatch is the starting point for this episode: not "poor circulation" as a throwaway answer, but a closer look at how warmth is made, carried, and moved.

We use the image of a woodstove at the end of a hallway to explore three traditional ideas: Yang as the fire, Blood as the carrier, and Qi as the movement. Then we walk through the five classical patterns behind cold limbs: Yang deficiency, Blood deficiency, constrained Qi, Cold-Damp blockage, and Blood stasis. We also separate Yin deficiency and a simple weather chill from the internal patterns they can resemble.

The episode closes with a seven-day observation exercise, the findings that deserve a medical conversation, and the safety limits around aconite formulas and hot foot soaks. This is a traditional framework for paying attention, not a diagnosis from a pair of cold toes.
